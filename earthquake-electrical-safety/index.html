Electrical Safety After an Earthquake

After the 4.6 earthquake near Boulder Creek, Central Coast homeowners need to check more than just cracks in the walls. Earthquake damage to your electrical system isn't always obvious — and it can be dangerous when it's not.

Earthquakes — even moderate ones like the 4.6 that struck near Boulder Creek in April 2026 — can cause electrical hazards that aren't immediately obvious. The shaking stress connections, shifts panels, and can loosen wiring that was already aging. Before you assume everything is fine, run through these checks.

If Any of These Are True — Leave the Main Breaker Off and Call Us

  • You smell burning near any outlet, switch, or the panel
  • You see scorch marks around outlets, switches, or the panel cover
  • You hear buzzing or crackling from the panel
  • You smell gas anywhere in the home

Start With Your Electrical Panel

Open your breaker panel and look for tripped breakers. If multiple circuits tripped during the quake, that's the panel doing its job — the earthquake's vibration can trip breakers mechanically. Reset them one at a time. If a breaker immediately trips again, don't force it. There's a fault on that circuit that needs professional diagnosis before you use it.

Listen to the panel with the door open. Buzzing or crackling is a sign of arcing — a loose connection that can cause a fire. Any burning smell from the panel means turn off the main breaker and call an electrician.

Older Panels: Higher Risk After a Quake

If your home has an older panel — especially Zinsco, Federal Pacific (FPE), or any panel more than 30 years old — seismic shaking is exactly the kind of stress that pushes aging components over the edge. If you lost power to any circuits and have an older panel, don't wait to have it inspected.

Outlets That Stopped Working

If specific outlets stopped working and there's no tripped breaker, look for tripped GFCI outlets nearby. These are the outlets with the test/reset buttons — usually found in bathrooms, kitchens, garages, and exterior locations. An earthquake can trip them mechanically without any electrical fault.

Press the reset button firmly. If power restores, you're fine. If the outlet still doesn't work after resetting all GFCIs on that circuit, you likely have a loose connection somewhere in the wiring — a job for a licensed electrician.

Check Visible Wiring in the Attic and Crawl Space

If you have accessible attic or crawl space, carefully look for:

  • Wires that have been pulled taut or yanked from junction boxes
  • Conduit or raceways that have shifted position
  • Junction box covers that have popped off
  • Any wire with damaged or cracked insulation

Do not attempt to reconnect any wiring yourself. If you find displacement or damage, document it with a photo and call us.

Homes Built Before 1980: Special Concern

Central Coast homes built before 1980 may have aluminum wiring, knob-and-tube wiring, or original panels that were already at or past their service life. These systems are far more vulnerable to earthquake stress than modern wiring. Aluminum wiring connections in particular are prone to loosening over time — seismic vibration accelerates this.

If your home is older and you lost power to any circuits, noticed flickering, or heard unusual sounds from your panel — even briefly — have it looked at before treating it as normal.

Quick Post-Earthquake Electrical Checklist

  • Check panel for tripped breakers — reset one at a time
  • Listen and smell at the open panel — no buzzing, no burning
  • Check all GFCI outlets (bathrooms, kitchen, garage, exterior) — reset if tripped
  • Test all outlets — note any that don't work after GFCI reset
  • Look at attic/crawl space wiring for displacement (if accessible)
  • Check outdoor outlets, landscape lighting, and EV charger connections
  • If anything is wrong — turn off the main, call a licensed electrician

What About My EV Charger or Solar System?

EV chargers and solar/battery systems have their own disconnect points. After an earthquake, check that the charger is still securely wall-mounted and that the circuit breaker dedicated to it hasn't tripped. For solar systems, the inverter may have shut down automatically — this is normal and safe. Follow your inverter manufacturer's restart procedure, or call us if you're unsure.

When to Call a Licensed Electrician

Call Arch Electric if you experience any of the following after the earthquake:

  • Breakers that trip immediately after resetting
  • Outlets that don't work even after GFCI reset
  • Flickering lights or dimming when appliances turn on (new symptom)
  • Any burning smell, buzzing, or visible scorch marks
  • Wiring that appears displaced in the attic or crawl space
  • Any concern about an older panel or aluminum wiring
  • You just want peace of mind — that's a valid reason too
